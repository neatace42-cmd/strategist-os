# Claims guide: read before writing any hook, headline, script or brief

Not legal advice. When a claim feels close to the line, flag it in the brief as **"needs compliance check"**.

## The 5 hard bans (from the research, §0.4)
1. **"Safe to take with your GLP-1"**, or any interaction claim. Say: "check with your doctor about your routine."
2. **"Reverses / stops / treats Ozempic hair loss"**. Treating a drug's side effect = a drug claim.
3. **"Clinically proven"** about the finished product. Our evidence is ingredient studies, not a product trial.
4. **"First / only GLP-1 hair supplement"**. It is false and invites fact-checks.
5. **Attacking minoxidil's efficacy.** Talk about dependency and the "dread shed" instead.

## Three rule sets, why each matters
**FTC (US advertising law): every claim needs proof.**
- Only "structure/function" wording: *supports, helps maintain, nourishes, promotes healthy-looking*. Never *treats,
  cures, prevents, reverses* a condition (telogen effluvium and alopecia are conditions).
- Ingredient vs product: the pumpkin-seed-oil hair-count study was on **men** and on the **ingredient**. Never present
  it as what our product does for women. Use it only as ingredient education.
- Testimonials must be real, typical, and disclosed if paid or AI-made. Never invent one (no GLP-1 testimonials exist yet).
- AI-generated people in ads: disclose them (some US states now require it).

**FDA (supplement labelling).** Structure/function claims need the standard disclaimer on the product and site
("These statements have not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration…").

**Meta (ad platform).**
- **Personal attributes:** never assert the viewer's health or medication state. ❌ "Losing your hair on Ozempic?"
  ✅ "Why so many women on a weight-loss journey are seeing thinner hair."
- **Weight loss / body image:** no before/after **body** images. Keep the creative on hair, not on weight.
- **Hair before/afters:** no zoomed scalp or bald-spot comparisons. Use thicker parted hair in normal life, follicle
  diagrams, ingredient visuals.
- **Prescription drugs and trademarks:** no drug-maker logos or implied partnership. In ads prefer the category
  ("GLP-1", "weight-loss injections", "the weekly shot"); brand names only descriptively, ideally in the advertorial.

## Translation table (use this)

|---|---|

| Ozempic-approved, or any implied affiliation | (leave it out) |

| Are you losing hair on Wegovy? | Why weight-loss-journey shedding happens, and the natural approach women talk about |
| Cures / treats telogen effluvium or alopecia | Supports / helps maintain / promotes |

## The load-bearing caveat
The estrogen → DHT story is **education, not a proven cause.** The mainstream clinical view of GLP-1 shedding is
telogen effluvium. Accept the nutrition truth first, then reveal the hormonal layer. Never write "the real cause is DHT"
as a fact.

## Before a brief ships: quick check
- [ ] No hard-ban phrase, no disease word (treat, cure, reverse, prevent)
- [ ] Viewer's health or medication state is not asserted
- [ ] No body before/after, no zoomed scalp
- [ ] Every number has a source; ingredient studies framed as ingredient studies
- [ ] Customer lines are real quotes (with source) or clearly marked as written lines
- [ ] Open items for the owner: risk tolerance on drug names, ad-account flag history (`running-notes/missing-context.md`)
